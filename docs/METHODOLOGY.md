The prototype will be deployed on the pig farm for a 19-hour monitoring period. The USB RGB camera and FLIR Lepton 3.1R thermal camera will capture images of the pigs every 30 minutes [1]. The 30-minute interval was chosen as a proposed testing parameter to reduce the Raspberry Pi 4's processing workload while still collecting periodic behavioral and thermal data.

The RGB images will be processed using the YOLOv8A-SD model to identify individual pigs and monitor their posture and behavior, including prolonged lying. Meanwhile, the thermal images will be analyzed to measure the pigs' infrared surface temperatures.

Throughout the 19-hour monitoring period, the system will record the timestamp, pig identification number, detected posture, estimated lying duration, surface temperature, behavioral status, and thermal status for each monitoring interval. After the 19-hour period, the system will evaluate the collected results for each pig to deterime whether abnormal behavior, such as prolonged or excessive lying, occured during the monitoring period. It will also check the pig's most recent temperature reading against the study's specified abnormal temperature range of 36.7 degree Celsius. If a pig exhibits abnormal lying behavior during the monitoring period and its final recorded temperature falls within this range, the system will send an SMS alert through the SIM800L GSM module. Otherwise, the results will be recorded without triggering an alert.

The collected data will be used to evaluate the prototype's monitoring performance and its ability to identify pigs exhibiting potential health-risk indicators. The system will serve as an early-warning screening tool and will not provide a definitive diagnosis of African Swine Fever (ASF).
